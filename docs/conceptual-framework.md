# Conceptual framework

IntuitionEDU treats academic agent work as a sequence of proposals, evidence, decisions and revisable experience.

## Human direction

A human specifies the purpose, who may work, what counts as evidence and what constitutes completion. An agent may propose a change; a proposal is not the human's decision. This distinction matters when work moves between chat, files and tools.

## Scoped collaboration

Responsibility is explicit at the task level. A solo assignment stays solo unless its scope changes. A team assignment names who drafts, who checks, who synthesizes and who decides. Scope changes should be visible before dependent work continues.

## Evidence-bearing artifacts

A claim points to its supporting source or generated result. A review states what it inspected and what remains uncertain. A revised artifact records what changed and which earlier claim or method it supersedes.

Provenance helps locate evidence. It does not by itself show that a method is valid or that the evidence supports the claim.

## Revisable judgment

The term intuition refers here to compact, context-sensitive guidance drawn from prior experience. It is a design aim for supporting judgment, not a claim of human-like cognition or an established learning mechanism.

Facts, human-approved preferences, research knowledge and provisional lessons should be distinguishable. A lesson may influence how an agent approaches a task; it cannot create permission or turn an uncertain claim into a verified fact.

## Learning as an empirical question

Reflection can produce a candidate lesson. Persistence makes that lesson retrievable. Retrieval makes it available during a decision. Only later behavior and outcomes can show whether it helped. Those stages should not be treated as equivalent.

An evaluation should compare later work with a meaningful baseline, record prior exposure, and look for transfer beyond the example that produced the lesson. See [evaluation](evaluation.md).
